Topic 01

What is EBPP?

Electronic Billing Presentment and Payment is a digital system that lets organizations deliver bills online and lets customers review and pay them electronically.

📨

Present

The bill or statement is delivered through a website, banking portal, email notice, or mobile channel.

👁️

Review

The customer checks the amount, due date, line items, and payment options before authorizing payment.

💳

Pay

Payment is initiated electronically, followed by confirmation and account reconciliation.

Notes basis: page 5. Expanded definition supported by Federal Reserve sources.

Common industry framing

Two broad arrangements

  • Biller-direct: the customer views and pays at the provider’s own site or app.
  • Aggregator / bank-consolidator: bills from multiple providers appear in one portal.

This simplified framing is added from external reference material.

Why it matters

EBPP creates value on both sides

⚡

Faster cycle

Digital delivery, payment, and confirmation reduce delays compared with postal billing and mailed checks.

🧾

Better records

Electronic statements and receipts improve searchability, tracking, and reconciliation.

🌍

Anywhere access

Customers can review and pay through connected devices without visiting a physical counter.

💰

Lower handling cost

Organizations can reduce printing, postage, and manual processing.

🔁

Recurring options

Many systems support saved methods, scheduled payments, and automatic payment instructions.

🔐

Control and security

Authentication, authorization, secure connections, and audit trails protect the transaction process.

Behind the screen

Who participates in an EBPP transaction?

🏢

Biller

Creates the bill, publishes it, accepts payment instructions, and updates the customer account.

👤

Customer

Receives the bill, verifies the details, chooses a method, and authorizes payment.

🖥️

Portal or aggregator

Displays one bill or combines several billers in a bank or consolidated dashboard.

🔌

Payment gateway

Securely passes payment information and responses between the front end and processing institutions.

🏦

Financial institutions

The payer’s and biller’s banks authorize, clear, and settle the electronic transfer.

📚

Accounting system

Matches the incoming payment to the invoice and maintains the audit trail and receipt.

Important distinction:

A simple money transfer is not automatically EBPP. EBPP includes electronic bill presentation or invoice information, payment authorization, and usually confirmation or reconciliation.

Topic 02

What is e‑auctioning?

An e‑auction is an online competitive bidding process used to sell or allocate goods, services, or assets. Its central economic function is often price discovery.

Core idea

Rules determine the result

Who can see bids, whether price moves up or down, and what the winner pays all depend on the auction format.

1. Seller lists an item
2. Auction rules are published
3. Bidders compete online
4. Winner and price are determined
5. Payment and fulfillment follow

English auction

Open ascending bids. The price rises until no one is willing to bid higher. The highest remaining bidder wins.

Auction logic

Why does each auction behave differently?

📈

English logic

Public competition pushes the price upward. When nobody is willing to bid more, the highest bidder wins and pays the final bid.

Open ascending
📉

Dutch logic

The seller starts high and lowers the price. The first buyer who accepts stops the auction, so speed matters.

Open descending
↔️

Double logic

Buyers submit bids and sellers submit asks. A trade happens only when a buyer's price can meet or beat a seller's price.

Bid meets ask
✉️

Vickrey logic

The highest bidder wins because they value the item most. They pay the second-highest bid because that is the price needed to defeat the closest rival.

Highest wins, pays second
🧠

Why not pay the highest bid?

In a sealed auction, paying your own bid can make people underbid. Second-price payment encourages bidders to write their true maximum value.

Truthful bidding
🏁

Simple Vickrey example

A bids 60, B bids 78, and C bids 52. B wins because 78 is highest, but pays 60 because 60 is the next best competing bid.

Winner ≠ payment amount

Reference examples

Where auction formats appear

🛒

eBay auction-style listing

Seller chooses a starting price. Buyers bid against one another, and when the listing ends the highest bidder wins. eBay also offers automatic bidding, where the system raises a bidder’s offer up to a private maximum.

English-style ascending auction
🌷

Dutch flower-market idea

A high asking price is progressively reduced until a buyer accepts it. This is the classic descending-price model associated with Dutch auctions.

Dutch auction
📊

Exchange order book

Many buyers submit bids and many sellers submit asks. Compatible orders are matched, illustrating the logic of a double auction.

Double auction
🔒

Sealed procurement exercise

In a classroom Vickrey example, each supplier submits one private bid; the strongest bid wins under the stated rule, while the transaction price is based on the second-best bid.

Vickrey mechanism

eBay is a documented working example. The other cards explain standard textbook settings and mechanisms; a real platform may modify rules.

Worked auction example

How an eBay-style auction works

1. Seller lists a camera with a starting price of $100
2. A enters a maximum of $140; the visible bid rises only as needed
3. B enters $155; the system automatically competes within each maximum
4. C enters $150; B remains highest because B's maximum is larger
5. At closing, B wins at the amount determined by the bid increment rules, not necessarily $155
Automatic / proxy bidding

Maximum bids

A · $140
B · $155
C · $150

The highest maximum controls the lead, but the displayed price is generally increased only enough to remain ahead, subject to the platform’s increments and rules.

Compare

Same digital environment, different purpose

EBPP

Goal: settle an obligation

  • Starts with an existing bill
  • Customer reviews a stated amount
  • Payment closes or reduces the balance
  • Success means accurate, secure processing
E‑auction

Goal: discover or allocate a price

  • Starts with an item, service, or asset
  • Participants submit competitive bids
  • Rules choose the winner and price
  • Success means a valid, transparent outcome
