02What is virtual currency?

A digital representation of value that exists only in electronic form — no physical note or coin.

  • Stored and transacted through designated software, mobile or computer applications
  • Transactions occur over secure dedicated networks or the Internet
  • Issued by private parties or groups of developers — not a central bank
  • Therefore not legal tender, and mostly unregulated

03Digital ⊃ Virtual ⊃ Crypto

Digital Currency
Any money in electronic form — including the balance in your bank account
Virtual Currency
Privately issued, not legal tender — game coins, loyalty points, platform wallets
Cryptocurrency
Uses cryptography + blockchain — Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT
Exam line: Virtual currency is a subset of digital currency, and includes cryptocurrencies and tokens issued by private organisations.

Question 1
Why is virtual currency needed in e-commerce?

E-commerce is global, digital and 24/7. Traditional payment rails are national, paper-derived, and built for face-to-face retail.

05Three structural gaps

a) Commerce is borderless — banking is not

A seller in Nepal and a buyer in Germany hit currency conversion, correspondent-banking delays, and cards that may not be internationally enabled. Virtual currency is natively global.

b) Cards were never designed for the Internet

The Cardholder Not Present (CNP) problem — no card impression, no signature. This forces the heavy five-party clearing cycle.

c) Digital goods need digital-native money

Selling a game skin, an article, a track or an API call means payments of Rs 5 or $0.05 — which cards cannot process economically.

06Collapsing the payment chain

Credit card — five parties, days to settle

Consumer→ Merchant→ Clearinghouse→ Merchant Bank→ Issuing Bank

Virtual currency — near peer-to-peer, minutes to settle

Consumer wallet→ Network→ Merchant wallet
Every removed intermediary is a removed fee, a removed delay, and a removed point of failure.
Question 2
What problems does it solve?

Eight concrete pain points in online commerce.

07Problems solved (1 – 4)

i) High transaction cost

The problemCards cost ~2–3% + a fixed fee on every sale, split among the clearinghouse, acquirer and issuer.
✓ Virtual currency solves it byRemoving those intermediaries — payment goes wallet-to-wallet, so the merchant keeps 2–3% more of every sale.

ii) The micropayment problem

The problemSell an article for Rs 5 when the card fee is Rs 4 and the business is impossible.
✓ Virtual currency solves it byMaking cost-per-transaction near zero — so pay-per-article, tipping, in-game items and metered APIs become viable. The strongest single argument.

iii) Slow settlement

The problemCards credit the merchant days later and bill the consumer on a monthly statement.
✓ Virtual currency solves it bySettling in minutes, 24/7 — the merchant has usable cash the same day, not next week.

iv) Chargeback & CNP fraud

The problemA buyer can dispute a card charge months later ("friendly fraud") and the merchant loses the goods and the money.
✓ Virtual currency solves it byMaking payments irreversible once confirmed — chargeback fraud simply cannot happen.

08Problems solved (5 – 8)

v) Exposure of card data

The problemEvery card purchase hands a card number to the merchant, building a database that can be breached.
✓ Virtual currency solves it byRevealing only a wallet address — no reusable credential ever leaves the buyer. Same privacy goal as SET's dual signature.

vi) Financial exclusion

The problemCard and bank penetration is low in developing markets, so a large share of people cannot buy online at all.
✓ Virtual currency solves it byNeeding only a phone and Internet — no bank account, no credit history, no issuer approval. It widens the merchant's addressable market.

vii) Locked out of global marketplaces

The problemBuyers without an internationally-enabled card cannot pay foreign merchants.
✓ Virtual currency solves it byBypassing the card network entirely — the same token works in every country, with no FX conversion chain.

viii) Weak retention & platform economics

The problemCash-out-at-checkout customers have no reason to come back; every sale must be won again.
✓ Virtual currency solves it byCommitting money to the platform when loaded — creating float, deferred revenue, repeat purchase and lock-in. Why Amazon, Steam and Daraz all issue their own currency.
Question 3
What is its importance in e-commerce?

Twelve points — exam answer format.

09Importance — 12 points

  • Faster transaction speed — settles in minutes, 24/7, no bank holidays
  • Lower transaction cost — fewer intermediaries, low interchange
  • Global reach — one currency for a worldwide customer base
  • Enables micropayments — unlocks digital-goods business models
  • Ease of use — a wallet app and an address; no card forms
  • Eliminates chargeback fraud — irreversible, protects the merchant
  • Privacy & security — no card credential shared with the merchant
  • Financial inclusion — serves unbanked and card-less customers
  • Less bank dependency — no acquirer gatekeeping
  • Programmability — smart-contract escrow releases payment on delivery
  • New business models — virtual goods, NFTs, metaverse, creator economy
  • Loyalty & marketing tool — coins and cashback drive repeat purchase

10Limitations & disadvantages

  • Unregulated — little or no legal recourse for buyers or investors
  • Can be hacked — exchanges and hot wallets are targets; a lost private key means permanent loss
  • Price volatility — a Rs 5,000 product may settle at Rs 4,200
  • Irreversibility cuts both ways — good against fraud, bad for consumer protection
  • Scalability & congestion — fees can exceed the value of a micropayment
  • Anonymity enables misuse — laundering, tax evasion, ransomware
  • Limited acceptance — weak network effects vs. cards
  • Legally prohibited in many jurisdictions — including Nepal

11The Nepal context

Cryptocurrency is illegal in Nepal. NRB declared Bitcoin trading illegal in August 2017 and reaffirmed the ban in later notices, grounded in the Foreign Exchange (Regulation) Act. Use, trade, investment and mining are all prohibited — and the ban remains in force in 2026.
  • Covers all private cryptocurrencies — Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT, and every altcoin or stablecoin
  • Rationale: monetary stability and anti–money laundering concerns
  • So in Nepali e-commerce, the practical virtual currencies are closed/platform currencies — wallet balances, loyalty points, coins — not cryptocurrencies

16What's next

CBDC — a digital Nepali rupee

NRB built a prototype in August 2025 and is refining it. Rollout will start with business use before the general public.

Key contrast: a CBDC is central-bank issued, regulated legal tender — the opposite of an unregulated private virtual currency. It could let people without bank accounts transact digitally.

Digital lending

NRB extended digital lending guidelines to MSMEs — short-term loans up to NPR 500,000 (1 year) and up to NPR 1 million longer-term.

Rising risk

Phishing, OTP scams, fake bank and wallet sites. Per NRB's Strategic Analysis Report 2024: one in two people involved in cyber-enabled financial fraud is aged 19–24, and one in three is a student.

17Key takeaways

  • Virtual currency is privately issued digital value — a subset of digital currency that includes cryptocurrency
  • E-commerce needs it because commerce is borderless, cards are not Internet-native, and digital goods need micropayments
  • It solves cost, settlement delay, chargeback fraud, data exposure and financial exclusion
  • Its costs are volatility, hackability, and the absence of regulation or legal recourse
  • Nepal has banned cryptocurrency but is booming on regulated rails — NPR 98.43 trillion (≈ 98 lakh crore rupees) in FY 2024/25, up 71%
  • The future here is a central-bank digital currency, not private virtual currency
