03How marketers improve ranking

🔑

Careful keywords

Choose words buyers actually type — e.g. “running shoes size 42 Kathmandu,” not vague jargon. Place them naturally in titles and content.

📝

Frequent content updates

Fresh blogs, product guides, and FAQs signal a living site. Search engines prefer pages that keep improving over abandoned catalogs.

🕷️

Readable by search engines

Clear HTML, titles, meta info, and structure so spiders can collect URL, title, and keywords — not a locked image-only brochure.

Nepal example A small electronics shop updates a post “Best budget phones under Rs 30,000 — 2026” with real specs. That keyword-rich, fresh page has a better chance to surface on Google than an empty homepage.

04Three parts of a search engine

1 · Spider
→
2 · Index
→
3 · Search utility

Spider

Auto-crawls the web and collects URL, title, keywords (often in meta tags), and info about other pages on the site.

Index

The store / database. Spider returns data; the index checks if already stored or updates it. Built for fast search.

Search utility

Takes the user’s terms, finds matches in the index, and generates a results page of URL links.

10On-page factors & techniques

Core factors (notes)

  • Internal linking — connect related product pages
  • Mobile friendliness — thumbs, not just desktops
  • Navigation — shoppers and spiders find paths
  • Content quality — useful, not spammy
  • Page speed — slow pages lose rank and carts

Techniques

URL optimization Tap
Short, readable URLs with keywords: /mens-running-shoes beats /p?id=99281.
Links in/out Tap
Internal links guide users across your site; thoughtful external links can support credibility when relevant.
Page content Tap
Place keywords where they fit: titles, headings, body — never spam. Every page needs a clear title.
High-quality content Tap
Guides, comparisons, honest specs. Analyze the whole site so weak pages get rewritten, not ignored.
Nepal tip Pokhara trekking shop: title “Gore-Tex jackets Kathmandu delivery,” fast mobile pages, and internal links from a “Dashain gift guide.”

14PageRank

Improve your rank · Set A

158 SEO activities (1–4)

1 Analytics2 Optimize pages3 Keyword content4 Diversify links 5 Images6 Mobile7 Speed8 Track
1 · Use Google Analytics Click
Track metrics: traffic sources, popular pages, bounce rates. You cannot improve what you never measure — Analytics shows which SEO efforts move the needle.
2 · Optimize existing pages Click
Fix titles, headings, thin content, and broken internal links on pages you already have. Often faster wins than only creating brand-new URLs.
3 · Create keyword content (not spam) Click
Write guides and product copy based on real keywords people search — without keyword stuffing. Helpful content ranks; spam gets punished.
4 · Diversify backlink portfolio Click
Earn links from varied, quality sources. Do not depend on one directory or one partner; a diverse mix looks more natural and resilient.
Improve your rank · Set B

168 SEO activities (5–8)

1 Analytics2 Optimize pages3 Keyword content4 Diversify links 5 Images6 Mobile7 Speed8 Track
5 · Optimize images Click
Add meaningful alt text, compress file size, and ensure images work on mobile. Heavy images kill page speed and accessibility.
6 · Mobile-friendly site Click
Most shoppers browse on phones. Responsive layout, tap-friendly buttons, and readable text are ranking and conversion necessities.
7 · Decrease load times Click
Faster pages keep users and please ranking systems. Compress assets, trim scripts, use caching — every second counts on Nepal mobile networks too.
8 · Track results Click
Close the loop: watch rankings, traffic, and sales after changes. Tracking tells you what to double down on and what to stop.
Full list from notes: Analytics → optimize pages → keyword content → diversify backlinks → images → mobile → speed → track.

19Data that powers suggestions

⭐

User ratings

Stars and thumbs teach the system what people loved or hated — classic Netflix-style signal.

🔎

Search queries

What shoppers type (“wireless earbuds”) reveals intent even before a purchase.

🛒

Purchase histories

Past orders predict future needs — T-shirt today, related clothing tomorrow.

Story You buy a cricket bat on Daraz. Next visit shows balls, gloves, and grips. Same idea as YouTube autoplay or Netflix “Because you watched…” — behavior in, suggestions out.
Why businesses invest

20Advantages of recommendation systems

  • Increased salesRelevant suggestions raise average order value and conversion.
  • Efficiency / fast searchShoppers find interesting items quickly instead of endless scrolling.
  • Customer retentionPersonalized emails and “for you” home screens bring people back.
  • Increased consumptionMore watches, more reads, more cart adds — Netflix/YouTube style engagement.
For e-commerce: too many products → hard to find interest → recsys become essential to sell, speed discovery, and retain customers. Large sites all use them.
Type 1

21Content-based filtering

  • Uses item features + user profile features
  • Past interest predicts future interest
  • Similar items are grouped by features (color, genre, brand, category)
  • Profiles come from history or by asking the user
Example You watched many cricket videos → YouTube suggests more cricket (same content features), even if your friends watch cooking.

Mental model

“Because this item is like items you liked before.”

Red cotton T-shirt
→
More red / cotton tees

Strength: works with item metadata. Limit: can feel repetitive if it only mirrors the past.

Type 2 · Most used

22Collaborative filtering

  • Most used approach; often better in practice
  • Powers experiences like YouTube and Netflix
  • Uses user interactions — who watched/bought/liked what
  • “People like you also liked…” instead of only item twin features
Content-based vs collaborative? Click
Content-based: match item attributes to your profile. Collaborative: match your behavior to other users’ behavior. Large platforms often blend both.

Mental model

“Because people like you chose this.”

You + similar shoppers
→
Shared picks

Needs interaction data. Cold-start (new users/items) is the classic challenge.

23Recommendation systems in e-commerce

Problem

Too many products — hard for customers to find what matches their interest.

Role

Recsys are essential: increase sales, help find items fast, retain customers (including via email).

Reality

Large sites all use them — Amazon-style “related,” Daraz homepages, streaming storefronts.

Homepage rails Tap
“Recommended for you” rows built from history and similar users.
Product page Tap
“Similar items” / “Frequently bought together” after a T-shirt view.
Email & push Tap
Retention: personalized offers so customers return instead of churning.

26Key takeaways
